# Realtime voice product engineering findings

Research note, 2026-05-04. Companion to `live-speech-models.md` (model landscape) and `polish-methodology-critique.md`. The brief: pull engineering practice from realtime/live voice product teams (blogs, technical reports, cookbook posts), focusing on findings that affect OpenQuack's pipeline design rather than another model leaderboard.

OpenQuack is **dictation, not conversation**: WhisperKit + optional LLM polish, asynchronous, single-user, push-to-talk-style. Many of the marquee findings from the realtime-voice space (barge-in, semantic VAD, full-duplex audio codecs, function-calling-during-speech) are tangential and are flagged as such throughout. The high-value findings are around cascade-vs-native-audio quality, on-device deployment engineering (Apple Silicon), eval methodology, and prompt structure for voice-adjacent LLM tasks.

## Cross-cutting lessons

- **Cascade still beats native speech-to-speech on reasoning by a wide margin.** On Big Bench Audio, GPT-4o text-to-text scores 92% vs GPT-4o Realtime speech-to-speech at 66% — a 26-point "audio reasoning gap." Speech-to-text-to-speech pipelines outperform native S2S with minimal degradation from the text baseline ([Evaluating Audio Reasoning with Big Bench Audio](https://huggingface.co/blog/big-bench-audio-release), 2024-12-20). For OpenQuack this directly validates the WhisperKit → text-LLM → text-out cascade — we are not leaving quality on the table by avoiding native audio.
- **Voice-to-voice latency targets cluster around ~800 ms; native S2S can theoretically hit 200 ms but production budgets are dominated by network + endpointing, not the model.** OpenAI's Latent Space writeup pegs ~500 ms US-client TTFB for the Realtime API, leaving ~300 ms for everything else inside an 800 ms target ([OpenAI Realtime API: The Missing Manual](https://www.latent.space/p/realtime-api), 2024-11-21). Moshi reports 160 ms theoretical / 200 ms practical end-to-end ([Moshi technical report, arXiv:2410.00037](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00037v2), 2024). Qwen3-Omni claims 234 ms cold-start first-packet ([Qwen3-Omni Technical Report, arXiv:2509.17765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17765), 2025-09-22). Doubao reports ~700 ms ([Doubao Realtime Voice Model](https://seed.bytedance.com/en/blog/doubao-realtime-voice-model-is-available-upon-release-high-eq-and-iq), 2025-01-20). These are conversational targets — not relevant to a finalize-on-release dictation UX, but they bound the latency cost of any future "live polish" feature.
- **Turn detection / endpointing is where most "latency" actually lives.** LiveKit: an 800 ms silence timeout "adds nearly a full second to every single response before the pipeline even starts" ([Turn Detection for Voice Agents](https://livekit.com/blog/turn-detection-voice-agents-vad-endpointing-model-based-detection), 2026-02-21). LiveKit ships a 135M-parameter SmolLM-v2 fine-tune for end-of-utterance prediction, CPU-resident, ~50 ms inference, 85% reduction in interruptions and 3% false-negative rate ([Using a transformer to improve end-of-turn detection](https://livekit.com/blog/using-a-transformer-to-improve-end-of-turn-detection/), 2024-12-20). For OpenQuack's push-to-talk model, this is a nudge: when we add hands-free, a small text-classifier endpointer on top of WhisperKit partials beats raw silence thresholds.
- **WebSockets are inadequate for production client-server real-time media; WebRTC is the right transport for browser/mobile clients.** Stated explicitly in the Latent Space writeup citing Pipecat's experience: TCP causes head-of-line blocking, no native bandwidth estimation, no automatic timestamping, robust reconnection is "very hard to implement" ([OpenAI Realtime API: The Missing Manual](https://www.latent.space/p/realtime-api)). Confirmed by OpenAI's own docs steering browsers to WebRTC and servers to WebSockets ([Realtime API with WebRTC](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/realtime-webrtc)). N/A for OpenQuack today (all-local, in-process), but the lesson generalizes: for any future remote-LLM polish, do not stream audio over WebSocket.
- **The teams shipping production voice agents are publishing structured-prompt templates, not vibes.** OpenAI's Realtime Prompting Guide prescribes an 8-section system prompt (Role & Objective / Personality & Tone / Context / Reference Pronunciations / Tools / Instructions & Rules / Conversation Flow / Safety & Escalation), prefers bullets over paragraphs, recommends ALL-CAPS for hard rules, and converts conditional logic into natural-language IF/THEN instead of pseudocode ([Realtime Prompting Guide, OpenAI Cookbook](https://cookbook.openai.com/examples/realtime_prompting_guide)). Direct lift target for OpenQuack's polish prompt structure.
- **Codec frame rate is the sequence-length lever.** Mimi at 12.5 Hz / 1.1 kbps / 80 ms frame size ([kyutai/mimi](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/mimi); [Neural audio codecs explainer](https://kyutai.org/codec-explainer)) drops audio from ~100 tokens/second (typical first-gen codecs) into LLM-tractable territory. Tangential to OpenQuack (we're not generating audio), but worth noting because it reframes the "native audio" pricing OpenAI publishes — the ~800 audio tokens/min figure for the Realtime API ([Latent Space writeup](https://www.latent.space/p/realtime-api)) implies a similar ~13 Hz effective frame rate.
- **Mixture-of-LoRAs is the production answer for "small base + multimodal."** Phi-4-Multimodal freezes the 3.8B language model and trains independent vision and speech LoRAs (~460M params for the audio LoRA) — ranks first on the OpenASR leaderboard despite the modest audio-side parameter count ([Phi-4-Mini Technical Report, arXiv:2503.01743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01743), 2025-03-07). Validates pattern of keeping a small frozen text base with a swappable per-modality adapter — relevant if OpenQuack ever ships a custom polish-LoRA on top of a stock Gemma/Qwen base.
- **WhisperKit's energy-and-latency wins come from Stateful CoreML + outlier-decomposed quantization, not from a smarter model.** 45% latency reduction (8.4→4.6 ms / forward pass on M3 ANE), 75% energy reduction (1.5W→0.3W), 1.6 GB→0.6 GB compression with <1% WER drop, all on Whisper-Large-v3-Turbo ([WhisperKit: On-device Real-time ASR with Billion-Scale Transformers, arXiv:2507.10860](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10860v1), 2025-07-15). This is the deployment bar OpenQuack inherits.

## Per-product

### GPT-4o Realtime / gpt-realtime (OpenAI)

The richest engineering material in the space, but most of it lives in the Latent Space "Missing Manual" writeup and the OpenAI Cookbook rather than OpenAI's first-party blog (which is marketing-shaped).

- **Finding:** Internal eval suite is **Big Bench Audio (reasoning) + MultiChallenge (instruction-following) + ComplexFuncBench (tool-calling)**. gpt-realtime scores 82.8 / 30.5 / 66.5 vs the December 2024 model at 65.6 / 20.6 / 49.7. — *Why it matters:* concrete, reusable bench taxonomy. Big Bench Audio's LLM-judge methodology is directly liftable for OpenQuack's polish bench (see below).
  - Ref: [Introducing gpt-realtime](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-realtime/), 2025-08-28.
- **Finding:** Realtime API session limits are **32,768-token context, 4,096 tokens/response, 60-minute session**. Truncation defaults to a 0.8 retention ratio to preserve prompt-cache hits. Cached audio tokens are 80% cheaper than uncached. — *Why it matters:* indicates how OpenAI thinks about audio-context cost; not directly relevant to local OpenQuack but informs any future remote-polish design (cache the system prompt + glossary aggressively).
  - Ref: [Developer notes on the Realtime API](https://developers.openai.com/blog/realtime-api), 2025-09-12.
- **Finding:** **`conversation.item.truncate` is required after barge-in** to align server-side context with the audio span the user actually heard. Streaming function calls are exposed but "not very useful" — you need the complete call before invoking. — *Why it matters:* tangential to dictation but a clear note that the realtime event-stream API leaks state into the application: a frame-processor (Pipecat) abstraction on top is what real users adopt.
  - Ref: [OpenAI Realtime API: The Missing Manual](https://www.latent.space/p/realtime-api), 2024-11-21.
- **Finding:** Default server-side VAD `silence_duration_ms` is 500 ms; production teams routinely raise it to 800-1000 ms for interview-style apps because OpenAI's VAD "seems more sensitive to background noise" than Pipecat's smoothed running average. — *Why it matters:* validates that endpointing is per-context-tunable, not a model property. For OpenQuack, push-to-talk sidesteps this entirely; for any future hands-free mode we should plan to expose a similar threshold.
  - Ref: [OpenAI Realtime API: The Missing Manual](https://www.latent.space/p/realtime-api).
- **Finding:** **Realtime system prompts have an 8-section recommended structure** (Role & Objective / Personality & Tone / Context / Reference Pronunciations / Tools / Instructions & Rules / Conversation Flow / Safety & Escalation). Bullets > paragraphs. ALL-CAPS for hard rules. Convert "if X then Y" into natural-language IF/THEN, not code. The model "strongly closely follows sample phrases" — example-driven beats abstract spec. — *Why it matters:* directly applicable to OpenQuack's polish prompt. Our current SPEC-007 polish prompts are unstructured; adopting at least Role / Reference Pronunciations / Instructions / Style sections would be a free win.
  - Ref: [Realtime Prompting Guide, OpenAI Cookbook](https://cookbook.openai.com/examples/realtime_prompting_guide), 2025.
- **Finding:** Voice-cloning is gated behind preset-voice-only deployment and per-session classifier-based content moderation in the Realtime API; voice cloning ("Voice Engine") remains in restricted partner preview with audio watermarking required and explicit-consent contractual obligations on partners. — *Why it matters:* not OpenQuack-relevant (we don't do TTS), but the watermarking precedent matters if we ever ship voice features.
  - Ref: [Navigating the challenges and opportunities of synthetic voices](https://openai.com/index/navigating-the-challenges-and-opportunities-of-synthetic-voices/), 2024-03-29; updates re-confirmed in [Introducing gpt-realtime](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-realtime/), 2025-08-28.
- **Finding:** `gpt-4o-mini-transcribe-2025-12-15` reports "~70% fewer hallucinations" vs prior `gpt-4o-transcribe`, "~90% fewer vs Whisper v2" against background noise / silence. Failure modes named explicitly: "long conversations or with edge cases like silence, and tool-driven flows." — *Why it matters:* hallucination-on-silence is a known WhisperKit pain too; for OpenQuack the practical implication is that a VAD prefilter before WhisperKit is worth more than picking a "smarter" model.
  - Ref: [Updates for developers building with voice](https://developers.openai.com/blog/updates-audio-models), 2025-12-22.

### Gemini Live (Google / DeepMind)

The blog material is shallower than OpenAI's. Engineering specifics surface mostly in Vertex docs, not the marketing post.

- **Finding:** Gemini 2.5 ships **two parallel architectures**: half-cascade (LLM emits text → separate TTS plugin) and native-audio (Talker emits audio directly). Half-cascade is being deprecated in favor of native audio for the production Live API. — *Why it matters:* Google ran the cascade-vs-native experiment in production and chose native — the opposite of Big Bench Audio's data. Reads as a UX decision (style-control, prosody, emotion) trumping a reasoning-quality regression. For OpenQuack the lesson is asymmetric: if you don't need expressive output, the cascade choice keeps reasoning quality intact.
  - Ref: [Advanced audio dialog and generation with Gemini 2.5](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-2-5-native-audio/), 2025-06-03; [Half cascade vs native audio (DeepLearning.AI forum)](https://community.deeplearning.ai/t/half-cascade-models-vs-native-audio-models/891460).
- **Finding:** Live API processes **audio input natively at 16 kHz** and **outputs at 24 kHz**; supports 24+ languages with mid-utterance language switching; trained to discriminate background speech from primary speaker. — *Why it matters:* ratifies 16 kHz as the de-facto input sample rate for production voice — same rate WhisperKit uses, so we're aligned. Language-detection-without-prompting is a lesson worth importing for OpenQuack's auto-language flow.
  - Ref: [Gemini Live API overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/live-api).

### Doubao Realtime Voice (ByteDance Seed)

Modest engineering depth on the public blog; the most useful piece is the eval methodology, which is a model for human subjective bench design.

- **Finding:** End-to-end Speech2Speech architecture with three-stage training: (1) interleaved-multimodal pretraining, (2) speech-text scaling, (3) RL post-training emphasizing emotional intelligence and safety. — *Why it matters:* validates the RL-on-emotional-targets pattern, but doesn't transfer to dictation polish.
  - Ref: [Doubao Realtime Voice Model — high EQ and IQ](https://seed.bytedance.com/en/blog/doubao-realtime-voice-model-is-available-upon-release-high-eq-and-iq), 2025-01-20.
- **Finding:** Subjective eval was **27 external testers across 10 cities, 800+ data points, age 21-33, 70% light Doubao users**. Doubao scored 4.36/5 satisfaction vs GPT-4o at 3.18/5; 2% of testers labeled it "too AI." — *Why it matters:* small-N, demographically constrained, externally recruited human eval is what they consider production-grade. SPEC-007's polish bench should plan for an analogous human pass on top of LLM-judge, scoped to ~25-30 raters.
  - Ref: same as above.
- **Finding:** "Ultra-low delay" claims (~700 ms TTFT in second-hand sources) are not numerically substantiated in the primary blog — the linked seed.bytedance.com/en/realtime_voice landing page describes the model qualitatively. — *Why it matters:* treat the 700 ms figure as marketing; no primary source. Document that gap rather than citing it.
  - Ref: [Realtime Voice (Doubao Team)](https://seed.bytedance.com/en/realtime_voice).

### Phi-4-MM / Azure Realtime (Microsoft)

- **Finding:** Mixture-of-LoRAs: 3.8B Phi-4-Mini base **frozen**, with per-modality LoRAs trained alongside encoders + projectors. Audio LoRA = ~460M params, ranks #1 on OpenASR leaderboard. — *Why it matters:* this is the cleanest published recipe for adding multimodal capability without losing the base text quality. If OpenQuack ever wants per-domain polish (legal, medical, code), a polish-LoRA on a frozen Gemma/Qwen base is the right pattern.
  - Ref: [Phi-4-Mini Technical Report, arXiv:2503.01743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01743), 2025-03-07.
- **Finding:** Single representation space across text/vision/speech is achievable with adapters — no separate models, no pipeline glue. — *Why it matters:* validates against the temptation to ship multiple specialist models when one frozen base with adapters can serve.
  - Ref: same.

### Apple speech (informs deployment)

The strongest findings for OpenQuack — these are direct competitors and direct collaborators (Argmax / WhisperKit) and the deployment bar is set here.

- **Finding:** **Apple SpeechAnalyzer is now the default Mac dictation API as of macOS 26**, modular (`SpeechTranscriber` + `SpeechDetector`), runs on Neural Engine + CPU, supports long-form audio, on-device, automatic language detection. Powers Notes, Voice Memos, Journal, system dictation. — *Why it matters:* this is the competitive ceiling for OpenQuack. We win on (a) Whisper's broader language support — Apple supports ~10 languages vs WhisperKit's ~100, (b) integration with LLM polish, (c) older-OS reach (iOS 17+/macOS 14+ vs Apple's iOS 26+).
  - Ref: [Bring advanced speech-to-text to your app with SpeechAnalyzer (WWDC25)](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/277/), 2025-06; [WWDC25 writeup](https://dev.to/arshtechpro/wwdc-2025-the-next-evolution-of-speech-to-text-using-speechanalyzer-6lo).
- **Finding:** Head-to-head on a 12-hour earnings22 subset: SpeechAnalyzer 14.0% WER @ 70x realtime; WhisperKit base.en 15.2% WER @ 111x realtime; WhisperKit small.en 12.8% WER @ 35x realtime. Both auto-download the model post-install; neither inflates app size. — *Why it matters:* WhisperKit-small wins on accuracy, WhisperKit-base wins on speed, Apple sits in between. For OpenQuack the implication is that "ship multiple Whisper sizes and let the user pick" is the differentiator vs Apple's single-model take.
  - Ref: [Apple SpeechAnalyzer and Argmax WhisperKit](https://www.argmaxinc.com/blog/apple-and-argmax), 2025-06-20.
- **Finding:** WhisperKit's deployment recipe: **Stateful CoreML for in-place KV-cache** (45% latency cut, 75% energy cut on M3 ANE); **Outlier-Decomposed Mixed-Bit Palettization (OD-MBP)** (1.6 GB → 0.6 GB, <1% WER regression); **block-diagonal attention with silence caching** in the encoder (612 → 218 ms, 65% cut); **LocalAgreement streaming** in the decoder for sub-second confirmed-text latency. Self-distillation on Common Voice 17 with modified attention masks. — *Why it matters:* this is the deployment bar OpenQuack inherits. We're using WhisperKit, so we get this for free, but we should be aware that any model-swap (e.g. distilling a domain-specific Whisper) requires repeating these steps to keep the energy budget intact.
  - Ref: [WhisperKit: On-device Real-time ASR with Billion-Scale Transformers, arXiv:2507.10860](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10860v1), 2025-07-15.
- **Finding:** **Speculative Streaming** (Apple ML Research): 1.8-3.1× decode speedup with ~10,000× fewer extra params than Medusa, no auxiliary draft model. Future-n-gram fine-tuning objective. — *Why it matters:* if OpenQuack ever runs a local polish LLM through MLX/llama.cpp, speculative streaming is the parameter-efficient win for resource-constrained Macs. The Medusa comparison is the relevant ablation.
  - Ref: [Speculative Streaming: Fast LLM Inference Without Auxiliary Models](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/llm-inference), Apple ML Research, NeurIPS 2024 ENLSP workshop.

### Other research-group findings

- **NVIDIA NeMo Parakeet-TDT-0.6b-v3 / Canary-1b-v2**: Fast Conformer encoder + TDT or CTC decoder, RTF on the order of 2e-3 (CTC, 30 s clip, A100 80GB). Granary dataset, ~1M hours / 25 European languages. *Implication:* the open-source ASR throughput frontier is server-side, not on-device. WhisperKit remains the right call for Mac. ([Pushing the Boundaries of Speech Recognition with NeMo Parakeet ASR Models](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/pushing-the-boundaries-of-speech-recognition-with-nemo-parakeet-asr-models/), 2024-04-18; [NVIDIA Releases Open Dataset, Models for Multilingual Speech AI](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/speech-ai-dataset-models/), 2025-08).
- **Mistral Voxtral Realtime (4B, Apache 2.0)**: Configurable streaming delay down to sub-200 ms, batch-equivalent quality at 2.4 s delay, within 1-2% WER at 480 ms. ~3x faster than ElevenLabs Scribe v2. 13 languages. *Implication:* an open-weights, edge-deployable streaming alternative to WhisperKit. Worth tracking — Voxtral Realtime's tunable-latency curve is exactly the API surface OpenQuack would want for a future "live polish" feature, where polish quality vs latency is a user-tunable tradeoff. ([Voxtral transcribes at the speed of sound](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-transcribe-2), 2026; [mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602 on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602)).
- **Mistral speech-to-speech assistant reference design**: cascade Voxtral Transcribe → Mistral Small → Voxtral TTS. Push-to-talk via keyboard shortcut, **not VAD**. PCM 24 kHz, 960-sample blocks. No barge-in. *Implication:* even Mistral's reference architecture for a voice assistant is push-to-talk, cascade, no barge-in — same shape as OpenQuack. ([Designing a speech-to-speech assistant](https://learn.mistral.ai/public/blogs/designing-a-speech-to-speech-assistant-2026-04-02), 2026-04-02).
- **Kyutai Moshi / Mimi**: 12.5 Hz / 1.1 kbps / 80 ms-frame audio codec, RQ-Transformer with K=17 streams (1 text + 16 acoustic), inner-monologue prefix aligns text and audio at frame level via Whisper-derived word timestamps. 160 ms theoretical / 200 ms practical end-to-end latency. *Implication:* sets the floor on what end-to-end native-audio latency can be when you control the whole stack. Tangential to OpenQuack but useful as the ceiling reference for any "live polish" experiment. ([Moshi paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00037v2); [Neural audio codecs explainer](https://kyutai.org/codec-explainer)).
- **Alibaba Qwen3-Omni (30B-A3B MoE)**: Thinker-Talker architecture, Audio Transformer encoder trained on 20M hours of supervised audio, 12.5 Hz token rate, multi-codebook MTP for residual codebooks, lightweight causal-ConvNet Code2Wav (replaces diffusion). 234 ms first-packet at single-concurrency, ~728 ms at 4-concurrent. Librispeech-clean 1.22 WER, multilingual Fleurs 5.31. *Implication:* Thinker-Talker is functionally the cascade pattern reified inside one model; the Talker is a streaming TTS conditioned on Thinker hidden states. Reinforces that "native S2S" production systems are closer to disciplined cascade than to truly unified models. ([Qwen3-Omni Technical Report, arXiv:2509.17765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17765), 2025-09-22).
- **LiveKit end-of-turn detection**: 135M-param SmolLM-v2 fine-tune, sliding 4-turn context, ~50 ms CPU inference, 85% reduction in interruptions, 3% false-negative rate, English-only, text-only (won't work with native-multimodal pipelines). *Implication:* a small text classifier on top of partials is the production answer to endpointing, not a bigger acoustic VAD. If we add hands-free dictation, this is the prototype to replicate — not raw silence thresholds. ([Using a transformer to improve end-of-turn detection](https://livekit.com/blog/using-a-transformer-to-improve-end-of-turn-detection/), 2024-12-20).
- **Modal+Pipecat 1-second voice-to-voice reference**: cascade of Parakeet-TDT-0.6b-v3 + Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 (vLLM) + Kokoro-82M TTS, all open-weight, separated stateful CPU bot from GPU inference, regional pinning, WebRTC client→bot, plain WebSocket bot→inference (over Modal Tunnels, bypassing the input plane). Hits median 1 s voice-to-voice when client and Modal are co-located. *Implication:* a reference implementation showing that 1 s open-weight voice-to-voice is achievable today *without* a native-audio model — pure cascade. Validates the cascade thesis. ([One-Second Voice-to-Voice Latency with Modal, Pipecat, and Open Models](https://modal.com/blog/low-latency-voice-bot), 2025-11-04).

## What this changes for OpenQuack

**Inference cadence — cascade is empirically validated, keep finalize-on-release as the default UX.** The 26-point Big Bench Audio gap between text-to-text (92%) and speech-to-speech (66%) is the single strongest argument for the WhisperKit→text-LLM→text cascade. It means the realtime-voice industry's marketing narrative ("native audio is the future, cascades introduce latency and lose nuance") is half-right at best — for *expressive output* (TTS prosody, emotion, accent) native audio wins; for *reasoning fidelity*, cascade still wins. OpenQuack's polish step is reasoning, not expressivity. The Modal+Pipecat reference implementation proves that even in the conversational regime — where native-audio's latency advantage is most visible — a disciplined cascade hits 1 s voice-to-voice. The implication for OpenQuack's roadmap: keep finalize-on-release as the default, and if we add live-polish later, do it as streaming-text polish on top of WhisperKit confirmed-text — not as a native-audio rewrite of the pipeline.

**System-prompt design — adopt OpenAI's 8-section structure for the polish prompt.** SPEC-007's current prompts are unstructured text. The Realtime Prompting Guide prescribes Role & Objective / Personality & Tone / Context / Reference Pronunciations / Tools / Instructions & Rules / Conversation Flow / Safety & Escalation, with bullets-over-paragraphs and ALL-CAPS for hard rules. For a dictation-polish use case the relevant sections collapse to: Role ("you are a dictation polish editor"), Reference Pronunciations (user's glossary of names/terms — we already have this in PolishCorpus), Style ("preserve the user's voice; do not change meaning; remove filler words; punctuate"), Hard Rules ("DO NOT add content. DO NOT translate. DO NOT change tense."). Worth a SPEC-007a follow-up restructuring our prompts and re-running the bench. The "model strongly closely follows sample phrases" finding also argues for a few-shot section in the polish prompt that we don't currently have.

**Eval methodology — the Big Bench Audio LLM-judge prompt template is directly liftable, and Doubao's 27-tester human eval is a borrow-target.** The Big Bench Audio judge prompt — "assess whether CANDIDATE ANSWER is CORRECT or INCORRECT, consistent with OFFICIAL ANSWER, ignoring spelling/synonym variation, judging the first answer if multiple are given" — is a clean template for the polish bench's LLM-judge harness. Our current SPEC-007 prompts the judge less rigidly than this. Three-run averaging is also their default. Separately, Doubao's human eval design (27 raters across 10 cities, 5-point scale, 800+ data points, recruited externally rather than from the team) is realistic for a hobbyist project — 25-30 raters is achievable through Prolific/MTurk for ~$200, and our current "Claude as judge" pass would benefit from a calibrating human pass at that scale. SPEC-007 should add a phase-3 human-eval rider once the LLM-judge bench is stable.

**Deployment-engineering — Apple SpeechAnalyzer is a real competitive threat, and we should call it out explicitly.** SpeechAnalyzer ships as a free OS-level API on macOS 26 with quality competitive with WhisperKit-base (14.0% vs 15.2% WER), zero app-size impact, and Apple's distribution. Where OpenQuack wins: language coverage (~100 vs ~10), older-OS reach (macOS 14+ vs 26+), the LLM polish layer that Apple has no equivalent for, and choice-of-model-size (we ship multiple Whisper variants; Apple ships one). The implication is that *the LLM polish step is the moat*, not the ASR — Apple will close the ASR gap quickly, but they don't have a polished local-LLM polish flow and won't, given their Apple Intelligence positioning. We should lean harder into the polish bench (SPEC-007/008) and the glossary/personalization angle (SPEC-008 phase-2). On the WhisperKit-internal engineering: OD-MBP quantization and Stateful CoreML KV-cache are now free — but if we ever distill a custom Whisper variant for domain dictation (legal/medical/code), we inherit the obligation to redo this conversion work. Budget ~1-2 weeks of CoreML conversion + benchmarking for any model swap, not a day. Speculative Streaming (Apple ML Research) is the equivalent free win on the LLM side if we run a local polish model — worth flagging to the inference-engine choice.
